# Competitive landscape

_Researched 2026-11-20 by web search. Prices come from store listings and
third-party articles, so check them on each vendor's own site before relying on them._

## Summary

Nobody is peanut-first. Free cards are generic PDFs; personalised cards come
with subscriptions or cost about $19 for a physical card. Translation quality is the
category's admitted weak spot, and parents and schools are underserved.

## 1. Free printable chef cards (nonprofits and government)

- **FARE**: free fillable PDF chef card, English plus several languages, aimed at travel and ethnic restaurants.
- **AAAAI**: free "Managing Peanut Allergy & Chef Cards" PDF.
- **Allergy & Anaphylaxis Australia**: chef cards in 21 languages via a certified translation service. Uses English where an allergen has no direct translation.
- **UK Food Standards Agency** and **Allergy UK**: chef cards and translation cards.

Trusted and free, but generic: you get a PDF, not something tailored to you.

## 3. Physical cards and merch

- **Zazzle**: peanut chef cards and badges, including Spanish.
- **Etsy**: digital downloads from other sellers at about $6–10; Equal Eats physical cards at about $18.99.
- **AchooAllergy**: business-card-size restaurant cards (peanut version) at $2.99.
- **Divoti**: custom metal bilingual allergy card at $18.99.

## 4. Medical ID cards (nearby, not head-on)

MedicAlert, Universal Medical ID, American Medical ID, ROAD iD, The ID Band Co.
All say a wallet card should **support** a bracelet, not replace it, because
first responders check the wrist and neck first. So we should position our
cards for restaurants, travel and school, **not as emergency medical ID**.

## 5. Kids and education

Mind Flight "Beyond a Peanut" flash cards; KidsHealth classroom activity cards.

## Gaps we can fill

1. **Peanut-first depth:** peanut oil (tolerance varies), groundnut and satay
   naming, the cuisines where peanut is common (Thai, Indonesian, West African).
2. **Free and personalised:** free custom generation plus one-off paid extras,
   vs generic free PDFs or subscriptions.
3. **Translation quality:** native-speaker review, shown openly on the site.
4. **Parents, schools and caregivers:** almost everything targets adult travellers.
5. **Digital:** phone wallet passes, or a QR code that opens the card in the reader's language.

## Market size

- About 2.9% of US adults report a current peanut allergy, at least 4.5M (Northwestern, 2021).
- Childhood peanut allergy is falling: prevalence dropped from 0.79% to 0.45% after early-introduction guidelines (CHOP, _Pediatrics_, Oct 2025).
